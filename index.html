Acoustic Metrology & Speaker Health: Engineering Guide

Evaluating a portable Bluetooth loudspeaker requires measuring physical parameters defined by international scientific standards. When checking a pre-owned or new speaker, listening to ordinary pop music masks subtle driver degradation due to dynamic compression and studio mastering. Pure tone waveforms and controlled stress diagnostics reveal mechanical fatigue, electrical shorts, and seal breaches.

International System of Units (SI) in Acoustic Metrology

Parameter SI Unit / Symbol Acoustic Significance Diagnostic Implication
Frequency ($f$) Hertz ($\text{Hz} = \text{s}^{-1}$) Vibrational cycles per second of the voice coil and diaphragm. Tests sub-bass ($20-60\text{ Hz}$), midrange ($250-4000\text{ Hz}$), and tweeter extension ($>10\text{ kHz}$).
Sound Pressure Level Decibel ($\text{dB SPL}$, reference: $20\ \mu\text{Pa}$) Logarithmic measure of effective sound pressure relative to human threshold. Measures dynamic headroom, maximum volume stability, and idle noise floor.
Continuous Power Watt ($\text{W} = \text{J}\cdot\text{s}^{-1}$) Root-Mean-Square True thermal capacity of the internal amplifier into the driver load. Continuous RMS rating indicates real output, unlike deceptive "Peak/PMPO" figures.
Total Harmonic Distortion Percentage ($\%\ \text{THD}$) Ratio of unwanted harmonic distortion energy to fundamental frequency energy. $\text{THD} > 1\%$ indicates amplifier clipping, voice-coil bottoming, or thermal saturation.
Audio-Visual Latency Millisecond ($\text{ms} = 10^{-3}\ \text{s}$) Transmission, decoding, and DSP buffer time between transmitter and speaker. Bluetooth latency $>250\text{ ms}$ makes gaming and movie dialogue unsynchronized.
Electrical Impedance ($Z$) Ohm ($\Omega = \text{V}\cdot\text{A}^{-1}$) Combined resistance and reactance across speaker voice coils. Voice-coil shorts reduce impedance, triggering thermal shutdown or distortion.

Common Causes of Failure in Used Bluetooth Speakers

1. Voice Coil Alignment Rubbing: When a speaker is dropped or overdriven, the cylindrical coil of wire suspended inside the permanent magnetic gap can warp. At high excursions (low bass), this warped coil rubs against the metal pole piece, producing a harsh raspy scratch.

2. Passive Radiator Gasket Splitting: Compact Bluetooth speakers rely on passive radiators to synthesize deep bass from tiny enclosures. The rubber surrounds fatigue from UV exposure, moisture, or age, developing micro-tears that leak air pressure and cause flapping sounds during bass notes.

3. Battery Cell Voltage Sag: Lithium-ion cells degrade after 300–500 cycles. When weak batteries cannot supply high peak currents for bass beats, the amplifier's power rail collapses, causing sudden shutdowns, Bluetooth disconnections, or heavy audio crackling at high volume.

About AcoustiScan

AcoustiScan is an open acoustic metrology tool designed to help consumers evaluate audio hardware before purchase. By utilizing the browser's native Web Audio API, AcoustiScan generates pure mathematical waveforms directly from the digital-to-analog converter (DAC) without compression artifacts.

Acoustic Methodology

All waveform generators employ 32-bit floating-point precision oscillators. Frequency sweeps utilize logarithmic frequency progression corresponding with ISO 226 human loudness curves.

Intermodulation Distortion testing uses the SMPTE standard mix (60 Hz low carrier paired with 1 kHz analytical tone) to detect chassis resonance and amplifier saturation.
